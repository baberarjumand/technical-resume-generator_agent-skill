### 8.1 ATS note on the STEMConnect page

Resumes are often screened by ATS before a recruiter. Yale’s tips:

- Avoid **tables, graphic images, or other complex formatting**
- Use **bullet points** in work history
- Use **Accomplishment Statements** aimed at skills the target job values
- **WHO method** for each bullet: **What** did you do? **How** did you do it? What was the **Outcome**? Quantify when possible
- Highlight relevant skills/accomplishments for **this** job — that may mean **leaving accomplishments off**
- In general **3–5 bullets** per experience *(the Work Experience section on the same page says **3–4**; the Formatting page says **no more than 3–4** and **1–2 lines** each — prefer 3–4 × 1–2 lines unless a senior role needs a fifth)*

### 8.2 Header

Must: **Name, email, phone, city and state.** Street address **not** necessary.

STEMConnect: students **should use Yale email**; if not, use a professional address; professional voicemail.

Optional: LinkedIn, personal website/portfolio, GitHub — “to showcase samples of your projects, skills, and abilities.”

**Template contact line (verbatim shape):**

> FIRSTNAME LASTNAME  
> New Haven, CT | Phone | Yale email | LinkedIn (optional) | GitHub (optional) | Personal Website/Portfolio (optional)

**Tension:** Formatting page (§8.8) recommends `first.last@gmail.com` (or other webmail) and makes city optional. TIH/FTL also prefer a personal Gmail you will keep. For an alum, not a Yale student): **personal Gmail**, not a school address.

### 8.3 Summary (optional)

3–5 **lines** (not TIH’s &lt;50 words / &lt;10-word headline). Tell how skills and experience make you a fit. Highlight key skills for **this** posting. Template: quantify relevant experience; tailor with **JD keywords**; may mention education, certifications, experience, and key skills.

**Examples on the page**

- Undergrad: “Team-focused software engineering undergraduate student looking for an entry-level job in X industry. Experienced with Python, C++, and MATLAB programming languages. Looking to apply coding skills and creativity in educational software development.”
- Experienced EE: “Driven and detail-oriented Electrical Engineer with 4+ years of experience with the design and fabrication of integrated circuits. Excellent troubleshooting, problem-solving, and analytical thinking skills. Solved a long-standing testing bug at Company X which increased the average testing time by 17%.”

The second example is closer to TIH (years + specialty + a quantified win). The first is an **objective** (“looking for…”) — FTL says avoid generic objectives. For an experienced candidate use the second shape, not the first.

### 8.4 Education

Most recent first, then older. First-years/sophomores may list high school; juniors/seniors “may not need” it (template note). Study abroad and other-university coursework can go here.

Optional: **Relevant Coursework** = *advanced* major courses that complement the target field (not a dump of intro classes). **Awards** here, or a separate awards section **immediately after education** if there are many.

Template:

> Yale University, New Haven, CT  Expected Graduation 20XX  
> BA/BS in XX  
> Relevant Coursework: (optional)  
> Awards: (optional)  
> High School Name, City, State  Graduation Year  
> Awards: (optional)

Formatting page add-ons: degree, program, anticipated grad date; study abroad; dissertation/thesis; **GPA is optional** unless a company asks — talk to an advisor if unsure. (TIH: only if &gt;3.50/4.00.)

### 8.5 Technical Skills

Hard skills from self-study, coursework, internships: languages, industry tools/machines, research/lab techniques, other expertise. **Match this section to JD key skills.**

Template note: **this section can go here (after education) or at the bottom.** Suggested labels:

- **Computer Languages:** e.g. Python, Java, C, C++, LabVIEW, MatLab, PHP, SQL, HTML, CSS, XML
- **Tools:** e.g. Keras, Tensorflow, Git, DynamoDB, Cognito
- **Skills:** e.g. Strategic Trading Systems, Machine Learning, Artificial Intelligence, Natural Language Processing

For experienced industry SWE (the candidate), TIH/Jachja still want skills **near the top**, after a headline/summary — not after education and not only at the bottom.

### 8.6 Work Experience

Paid **or unpaid**, including jobs **outside** the target industry if bullets make skills transferable. Use **industry competencies** (NACE PDF + the JD). Reverse chronological.

Each role: **3–4 bullets** of duties **and impact**. Present tense for current jobs, **past tense** for previous. Formatting page: **present simple** (`create`), **not** present continuous (`creating`).

**Template header formats**

> Employer, Your Title, City, State  Dates  

Or stacked titles at one employer:

> Employer, City, State  Dates  
> Job Title (dates)  
> Job Title (dates)

The skeleton has **no sample bullets** — fill using WHO / APR / X-Y-Z (§8.1, §8.9).

### 8.7 Projects & Leadership; interchangeable sections

Flexible catch-all for:

- Class **final projects** (tech + teamwork/problem-solving)
- Student clubs / professional orgs (leadership)
- Independent technical projects

Template:

> PROJECTS & LEADERSHIP EXPERIENCE  
> Independent Technical Project Name, Independent Project  Dates  
> Class Project Name, Class Project, Class Title  Dates  
> Yale Student Organization, Your Title, City, State  Dates

**Research Experience:** separate section for on-campus lab work, formatted like work experience.

**Volunteer Experience:** if work history is thin, treat volunteer roles as work experience (same bullet style). Show communication, teamwork, project management, etc.

### 8.8 Related — Resume Formatting and Common Errors

- **Canonical URL (404 live as of 10 Sep 2026):** [https://ocs.yale.edu/resources/resume-formatting/](https://ocs.yale.edu/resources/resume-formatting/)
- **Wayback used:** [2026-03-25 snapshot](https://web.archive.org/web/20260325001359/https://ocs.yale.edu/resources/resume-formatting/) and 2026-04-21 capture; page **published 27 Jul 2022**, **modified 31 Mar 2026** (same day as STEMConnect refresh)
- Also linked from [OCS resume templates](https://ocs.yale.edu/resources/ocs-resume-template/)
- Archived extra file: [2016 Resume Action Verbs PDF](https://cdn.ocs.yale.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/77/2020/05/2016_Resume_Action_Verbs.pdf) (superseded by the live verbs page)

**Claim:** average reader spends **20 seconds**.

**Layout**
- Font **10–12 pt**; name **12–14 pt**
- Standard fonts: **Times New Roman, Helvetica, Garamond**
- Margins **≥ 0.5"**
- **One page** (undergrad). Master’s **1–2 pages**; PhD/postdoc **2–3 pages max**. Longer only for research or performing arts — ask an advisor
- Black and white unless you know they print color
- Bold/italics/bullets in **moderation**; consistent size, underline, spacing, indents, bold, **date format**, spelling
- Check spelling/grammar; **avoid contractions, abbreviations, and slang**
- **Do not use pronouns**
- Reverse chronological where appropriate

**Header (this page vs STEMConnect):** professional email as `first.last@gmail.com` or other webmail; phone; street address unnecessary; LinkedIn personal URL under the name; city/state without zip if relevant: `New Haven, CT | Chicago, IL | email@yale.edu | XXX-XXX-XXXX | LinkedIn or website`.

**Work bullets:** action verbs aligned to the JD; past vs present simple; **≤3–4 bullets**; **≤1–2 lines** each.

**Leadership & Community:** same formatting as work; skills, duties, quantified results.

**Publications & Presentations (optional):** more for grad/postdoc; only the most relevant; not exhaustive for non-academic jobs.

**Skills:** computer, language, lab, performances as the field requires. **Languages: proficient / advanced / fluent / native** (Yale allows proficiency labels for *spoken* languages; Jachja forbids them for *tech* skills). Programming: name specific software (stats, comms/media, video editing).

**Licenses/certs:** name + expiration if any.

**How many versions:** tailor by function/industry. Consulting values PM/leadership/analytics; research values technical knowledge and research experience.

**Common errors:** passive language; full-sentence narrative instead of action bullets; photo or personal data (marital status, sex, age, religion); listing *all* positions instead of relevant ones; visual clutter / no white space; no quantified results.

### 8.9 Related — Writing Impactful Resume Bullets

- **URL:** [https://ocs.yale.edu/resources/writing-impactful-resume-bullets/](https://ocs.yale.edu/resources/writing-impactful-resume-bullets/)

Accomplishment statements = quantified achievements from internships, student orgs, volunteer, military, or education — **not** a task list. Employers want contribution and value.

**Prompts before writing:** improved something; more with less; reduced cost; productivity; saved time; recruitment; designed/implemented a process/product; brought constituents together; morale; solved a problem; led a team; presented complexity clearly; multitasked; long hours / short deadline; balanced extracurriculars with coursework; took initiative; awards/reviews. Also: proudest work; what colleagues would say; how the org benefited; special-project outcomes; tangible evidence.

**Formula 1 — Action + Project + Result (APR)**  
Group tasks into a project → action verb for **your** contribution (not the team’s) + skills used → quantified result (% improve/increase). Mention publication or patent if that is the result.

> `[A] action verb + [P] project/problem + [R] results, quantified`

**Formula 2 — Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y] by doing [Z]**  
Lead with impact, then the number, then the method. (Same family as TIH’s action → quantifiable outcome and Jachja’s verb + outcome + tools.)

**Result-bullet before/after (verbatim shapes)**

| Before | After |
| --- | --- |
| Worked with a student leadership committee to increase member participation | Led a 5-person leadership team to increase student participation by 100% from 50 to 100 members by creating a stronger social media presence |
| Contributed to system for streamlined application submissions and tracking | Collaborated with the IT team to develop an online application submission and tracking system, reducing cost by 10% |
| Selected for scholarship | Selected as one of 230 participants nationwide, based on top class rank coupled with community engagement work |

Tips: stronger verbs (`led`, `created`, `developed`); numbers; **baseline for comparison** (50→100, not just “increased”).

**Skill-bullet before/after**

| Before | After |
| --- | --- |
| Explored the evolutionary origins… by studying capuchin monkeys | Delivered key insights on the evolutionary origins of cognitive processes by researching and analyzing over 100 years of literature on capuchin monkeys |
| Looked at branding and marketing strategies of Company X… | Compiled an overview of the competitive landscape for Company X by conducting expert interviews and online research |
| Worked with fellow interns to put on a conference | Facilitated collaboration among 5-person intern team to put on a conference; divided tasks based on expertise, developed a feasible timeline, and kept team on task and within budget |

Pitch transferable skills the next employer values; specifics show you can make an impact.

### 8.10 Related — Resume Action Verbs

- **URL:** [https://ocs.yale.edu/resources/resume-action-verbs/](https://ocs.yale.edu/resources/resume-action-verbs/)
- Instruction: go beyond “responsible for”; focus on what you **accomplished, improved, or changed**.

Categories on the page (use the **Technical Skills** and **Improved** lists first for SWE): Achieved; Communication; Created Something; Creative Skills; Customer Service/Support; Financial; Helping; Improved; Management; New (Client, Funding, Partner); Organizational Skills/Project Management; Oversaw; Research or Analysis; Teaching; Technical Skills.

**Technical Skills verbs:** Assembled, Built, Conserved, Constructed, Converted, Designed, Determined, Developed, Engineered, Enhanced, Fabricated, Implemented, Improved, Inspected, Installed, Overhauled, Programmed, Regulated, Remodel, Repaired, Restored, Serviced, Specialized, Specified, Standardized, Studied, Upgraded, Utilized.

**Improved (impact) verbs** include: Accelerated, Decreased, Delivered, Digitized, Enhanced, Increased, Maximized, Reduced, Streamlined, Transformed, Yielded, …

Do not dump the full communication/helping lists onto a SWE resume; pick verbs that match the bullet’s actual work.

### 8.13 Related — Using AI In Your Job Search (OCS channel; not linked from STEMConnect body)

- **URL:** [https://ocs.yale.edu/channels/using-ai-job-search/](https://ocs.yale.edu/channels/using-ai-job-search/)
- Course papers: follow instructor policy; do not paste AI into papers unless allowed. **Employment** resumes/cover letters: AI allowed under the guidance below. **Grad school / fellowships:** treat like course papers — do **not** apply AI.
- **AI fluency** (Veris Insights, as quoted): judgment (when to use), application (direct/refine), evaluation (critically assess). Employers look for this via hackathons, personal projects, communities — extra credentials not required. Some interviews watch you *work with* AI.
- Start with **your own draft**; AI improves, does not replace. Targeted prompts; try several phrasings.
- **Privacy:** strip name/identifiers except on Yale’s Big Interview subscription. Do not blindly accept edits; verify facts. Employers **may scan for AI** and reject copy-paste output — keep personal style.
- **Resume/cover prompts they publish:** highlight skills from the JD; extract top 15 JD keywords; find keywords missing from the CV; “I have expertise in X — suggest 3 bullet revisions.” Always start from original work.

### 8.14 Conflicts to remember vs earlier sources

- **Email:** STEMConnect = Yale email; Formatting + TIH/FTL = personal Gmail. experienced candidates → Gmail.
- **Summary length:** Yale 3–5 lines vs TIH &lt;50 words + headline-as-title. Prefer TIH density for experienced SWE; Yale’s EE example (years + specialty + 17% metric) is the usable shape.
- **Section order:** student template is Header → optional Summary → **Education** → Technical Skills → Work → Projects. Experienced SWE (TIH/Jachja): summary + skills + work, education later.
- **Bullet count/length:** 3–5 vs 3–4 vs Jachja 2–4 × ≤3 lines vs Yale formatting 1–2 lines. Working default: **3–4 bullets, 1–2 lines, verb + metric + tools**.
- **Proficiency labels:** Yale yes for **languages**; Jachja no for **tech** skills.
- **Abbreviations:** Yale formatting “avoid abbreviations”; TIH “spell out AWS as Amazon Web Services.” Do both: spell out on first use, keep the common acronym.
- **Times New Roman** is Yale’s template font; TIH prefers Arial/Calibri/Garamond. All are ATS-safe. Pick one family and stay consistent.
