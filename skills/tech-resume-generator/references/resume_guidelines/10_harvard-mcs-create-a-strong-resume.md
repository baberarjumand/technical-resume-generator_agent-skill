## 10.1 Quick Tips

### Resume language should be

- Specific rather than general
- Active rather than passive
- Written to express not impress
- Articulate rather than “flowery”
- Fact-based (quantify and qualify)
- Written for people who / systems that scan quickly

### Top five resume mistakes

1. Spelling and grammar errors
2. Missing email and phone information
3. Using passive language instead of “action” words
4. Not well organized, concise, or easy to skim
5. Not demonstrating results

### Don’t

- Use personal pronouns (I / We)
- Abbreviate
- Use a narrative style
- Use slang or colloquialisms
- Include a picture
- Include age or gender
- List references
- Start each line with a date

**On “Don’t abbreviate”:** the published samples still use `A.B.`, `GPA`, `SAT I`, `SQL`, `Jan.`, `Sept.` The rule is aimed at unexplained shorthand (`Mgr`, `asst`), not standard degree/test/tech tokens. TIH’s “spell out Amazon Web Services once, then AWS” still wins for an experienced candidate.

### Do

- Be consistent in format and content
- Make it easy to read and follow, balancing white space
- Use consistent spacing, underlining, italics, bold, and capitalization for emphasis
- List headings (such as Experience) **in order of importance**
- Within headings, **reverse chronological** (most recent first)
- Avoid information gaps such as a missing summer *(undergrad internship calendar — not a 6+ year industry gap rule)*
- Be sure formatting translates properly if converted to PDF

Sample-sheet extra annotations (from `resume-sample.pdf` callouts):

- Always use `@college` email and check it even if forwarding is on — **Harvard student only**. Experienced candidates: personal Gmail (existing default).
- If an employer **asks** for SAT/ACT or GPA, put them in Education.
- Use different verbs (don’t repeat the same opener).
- Relevant skills can be shown through campus/volunteer work, not only jobs.
- Be consistent presenting data: **either numerals or words, not both**. The generic sample still writes “fifty” vs numbers in places; the category sheet uses `30` / `75` / `20%`. Prefer numerals (TIH/Yale).

## 10.3 Using AI

Primary page + [AI for Resumes and Cover Letters](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/ai-resumes-and-cover-letters/) + the [AI professional-development channel](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/channels/ai-for-professional-development-and-exploration/). Aligns with Yale OCS (§8.13): **editor, not author**.

**Policy (all three pages):** Documents must authentically represent the candidate. Generative AI can brainstorm bullet revisions, pull JD keywords, and improve an existing draft. It **should not be the primary author** — output is “very generic.” Employers and admissions “can often recognize generic, overly polished, or impersonal AI-generated materials.”

### Resume process (AI page)

- **Start with your draft** — existing resume or raw details (impact, numbers). Prompt **section by section or bullet by bullet**.
- **Know the audience** — tailor to this employer; they may differ from generic industry advice.
- Treat generated text as a **suggested edit**. Verify accuracy. Do not implement blindly.
- **Know every line** for interviews. Read aloud for repetition/inaccuracy.
- **ATS:** many high-volume employers scan for keywords. Use **basic formatting**, **explicitly list skills**, and put **JD keywords in context** (not a stuffing dump). Optimization tools can show the “persona” a resume conveys.
- **Privacy:** do not share personal or proprietary data with generative AI. Channel adds: student records, research, employer materials, financials, PII; review each tool’s policy; Harvard is especially cautious with **public** AI systems.

### Cover-letter process (AI page)

- Paste the JD; ask for top skills/expertise.
- Ask the tool for resume examples that demonstrate those skills, then **add stories that are not on the resume**.
- Feedback prompt: “Does this letter convey my ability to add value to the company?” Grammar like Grammarly is fine.
- Read aloud: strong case, specific accurate examples, **your voice**, well-written, flows.
- **Follow employer rules** on whether AI may be used in applications. Channel: if disclosure is requested, be transparent.

### Channel-level cautions (apply to resumes)

- AI hallucinates facts, **credentials**, citations, employer info, requirements — verify.
- AI can reinforce bias.
- Start with your own work; iterate prompts; treat output as a draft.
- Combine with an MCS advisor (human).
- Watch AI-enabled job scams / fake postings.

### Example prompts (verbatim shapes)

**Resume:** ACTION VERBS (`great active verbs for [job title] resumes`); KEYWORDS (`top 10 keywords that an ATS would track` from a JD); ROLE PLAY career advisor (five things to include); ROLE PLAY recruiter (strengths + skill/experience gaps); REVISE (5 suggestions for one bullet given a JD); SKILL COMPARISON (fold listed skillsets into an experience description); SKILL MATCH (ideas to highlight a field of expertise).

**Cover letter:** SKILL IDENTIFICATION (top 3 JD skills vs resume, minus personal info → three experiences to highlight); SKILL SUMMARY; ORGANIZATION RESEARCH (mission, news, what is unique); WORDING JOB/EXPERIENCE GAPS (career-switch explanation); REVISION (5 versions matching the writer’s tone); ROLE PLAY recruiter on the letter.

### Big Resume (Harvard-only)

[Big Resume](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/big-resume/) = Big Interview **ResumeAI**: upload, ATS-oriented feedback in minutes. Register once with a **Harvard email**, then Harvard Key. **Not a substitute** for the candidate’s own language, details, and proofreading. Same product family as Yale’s Big Interview (§8.11). Unusable for an experienced candidate without a Harvard address; the “AI is feedback, not copy” rule still applies.

## 10.4 Fill-in templates (bullet vs paragraph)

Template pages: “Use this [bullet-point | paragraph] template to build out your first draft.”

**Shared skeleton (both formats)**

1. **Name** (centered)
2. Contact: `Home or Campus Street Address • City, State Zip • youremail@college.harvard.edu • phone number`
3. **Education** — Harvard first (degree, concentration, **GPA optional**, thesis optional, relevant coursework / awards optional) → study abroad if any → **high school** (GPA, SAT/ACT, honors “an employer may want to know”)
4. **Experience** — org, `City, State (or remote)`, title, Month Year–Month Year
5. **Leadership & Activities** — same block; “omit descriptions” allowed; **move above Experience** if more relevant to this opportunity
6. **Skills & Interests [optional]** — Technical (software/languages **and fluency**); Language (fluency); Laboratory if applicable; Interests “that may spark interview conversation”

**Bullet vs paragraph instructions (verbatim idea):** begin each line with an action verb; include details that show accomplishments, skills, knowledge, abilities, or achievements; **quantify where possible**; **no personal pronouns**; each line is a **phrase, not a full sentence**. Paragraph version adds: **avoid articles** so descriptions flow.

**Physical specs (2025 Word, preferred over Accessible)**

- US Letter; **Calibri** (ATS-safe). GDoc export also embeds Arial / Times.
- Bullet docx: body sizes mix **10.5 / 11.5 / 12 pt**; name ~12 pt. Paragraph similar (8 / 11.5 / 12).
- Margins (2025 bullet): ~**0.42" L/R**, 0.5" top, **~0.19" bottom** — tighter than Yale’s 0.5" floor. Accessible Word is **0.5" all sides** but uses **six Word tables**.
- **0 tables** in the 2025 files. **Prefer 2025 / GDoc**, not the Accessible table layout (TIH/Yale ATS).
- No graphics. Reverse chrono. Dates on the **right**, not starting the line.

**Harvard vs this repo’s experienced defaults**

| Harvard College template | the candidate / existing policy |
| --- | --- |
| Education first, high school on the page | Education last; **no high school** |
| Street / campus mailbox in the header | City only; no street (Yale/TIH) |
| `@college.harvard.edu` | Personal Gmail |
| Optional interests / lab / language fluency | Skip hobbies; no beginner/proficient **tech** labels (Jachja). Spoken-language fluency OK (Yale) |
| Paragraph format offered | **Bullets only** (TIH, Yale, Jachja, LinkedIn) |
| Skills & Interests last | Skills high (after headline) |

## 10.8 Cover letters

**Tips (page):** the letter is a writing sample and part of screening. Highlight skills/experiences most applicable; tailor to the organization.

Rules:

- Address a **specific person** if you can.
- Research, then tailor.
- Concise, factual, **≤ one page**. No flowery language.
- Examples that support skills.
- Reader’s shoes: what proves you can do the job?
- Don’t overuse **“I”.**
- Marketing tool — action words.
- MCS advisor feedback.
- Check PDF conversion.
- Reference JD skills/experiences and connect them to credentials.
- **Same font type and size as the resume.**

**Annotated skeleton** (`cover-letter-template.pdf`): date; complete name/title/street (optional if saving space); `Dear _____:` **(colon, not comma)**; opening (why writing, position, how heard; optional three-reason summary fit); middle (why this employer, 1–2 key examples, **do not reiterate the entire resume**, confident writing sample); closing (interest, contribute, thank, look forward to discussing); `Sincerely,` / typed name.

**Filled sample on the page (1 Sep 2026)** — Alex Crimson → Morgan Smith, Director of Communications, Jumpstart, 308 Congress Street, 6th Floor, Boston. Role: Marketing and Communications via Crimson Careers. Three paragraphs: (1) senior, History and Literature, excitement + skills offer; (2) Summer Urban Program senior counselor, 10 fifth graders, tailored STEM/reading lessons; (3) Director of Marketing, Social Innovation Collaborative — social strategy on Facebook/Twitter/Instagram → **20%** membership, **15%** social engagement. Thank / look forward. Despite the “don’t overuse I” rule, every paragraph is first person (normal for letters; the rule means don’t start every sentence with I).

## 10.12 Tensions with existing policy / experienced candidate

Harvard MCS is a **current-College, US liberal-arts** guide. Useful: quantify, action verbs, reverse chrono, headings by importance, PDF check, AI-as-editor, ATS = basic format + skills listed + keywords in context, same font on cover letter, named addressee.

Do **not** copy the student chrome onto the candidate’s industry résumé:

1. **Order:** Education-first + high school is for undergraduates. Experienced candidates: headline → contact → skills → work → education last.
2. **Contact:** street/mailbox and `@college` email are template defaults; the **Tech example already drops the street** and shows `github.com/user`. Use that + personal Gmail + city (or “Remote”) + LinkedIn.
3. **Paragraph format:** offered; this repo stays on **bullets**.
4. **Interests / SAT / high school / tool-fluency (beginner SPSS):** student optional. Skip.
5. **GPA:** templates say optional; samples show 3.55–3.73. Non-US / lower GPAs are rarely worth listing — omit unless a posting requires them.
6. **Skills placement:** CS annotation = after Education. Experienced SWE default remains **skills near the top**.
7. **Tables:** only in the Accessible Word files — don’t use.
8. **Margins:** 2025 Word is tighter than 0.5"; Yale/TIH **≥0.5"** still wins.
9. **Font:** Harvard templates = **Calibri** (fine). Annotated InDesign samples = Times. Either is on the ATS-safe list.
10. **Cover letter:** if one is requested, follow Harvard’s 3-part skeleton + metrics (20% / 15% in the Jumpstart sample) and TIH/Yale honesty (no invented numbers).

**Template match:** Harvard Tech example ≈ UNC CS Template #1 (intern). Harvard Engineering ≈ research-heavy STEM undergrad. Neither is the experienced FAANG-style stack.
