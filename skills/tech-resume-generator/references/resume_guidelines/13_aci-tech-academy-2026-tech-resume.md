## 13.1 Live 2026 guide — section table

Order they recommend for **breaking into IT**:

1. **Contact** — name, phone, professional email, LinkedIn, GitHub or project site. “Keep it simple.”
2. **Professional summary** — **2–3 sentences** on adaptability, tech readiness, customer focus, comfort with modern tools **including AI**.
3. **Skills** — technical mix *and* workplace skills (communication, organization). Examples they name: troubleshooting, networking basics, scripting, cybersecurity fundamentals.
4. **Experience** — reverse chronological, measurable, action verbs + numbers; **non-tech jobs count**.
5. **Education & certifications** — coursework, certs, degrees; **expected dates if in progress**.
6. **Projects** — tools + goal + outcome (problem-solving).
7. **Optional** — volunteer, leadership, languages, “anything that strengthens the story.”

**ATS:** keywords from the JD, **naturally**; standard headings; no decorative design; readable fonts. “Smarter ATS in 2026” is asserted, not evidenced.

**Career-changer translation:** customer service, task management, problem-solving, documentation, productivity software, workflow improvement — with numbers.

**Proofread:** typos still matter to humans “and robots.” Second pair of eyes.

**AI (their list):** keywords, **draft résumé sections**, targeted covers, interview prep, organize the search. Then: enhance, don’t replace your voice; “let personality shine.”

The rest of the post is Career Services / CareerConnect sales (mentor, AI résumé suggestions, mock interviews, curated beginner roles).

## 13.2 Karla Urbina — résumé basics (same-site Q&A)

Most usable ACI writing rules, aimed at IT/cyber/PM career switchers:

- Skills first: transferable **and** learned; **soft + technical / software / hardware** (they want soft skills *on* the résumé)
- **1–2 pages max**
- Call out any IT tasks inside old jobs
- Map experience to **this** JD
- Accomplishments, not duties-only
- Contact current and professional: **no full street address**; professional email; LinkedIn
- **Don’t be fancy:** no loud colors, pictures, or **columns**. “A basic **skills-based** résumé is best” for ATS
- Reviewers spend **seconds** — highlight what matters

Also: customer service is “the #1 transferable skill”; conflict resolution and collaboration next. First IT job may be a **bridge role** plus certs (~10 weeks in their pitch).

## 13.3 Skills-focused post + IT Career Prep Module 2

**Skills-focused** (first-job / portal algorithms):

- Tailor each application; **don’t** send one generic file to 100 listings (TopResume: **75%** never reach a human — vendor stat, same family as Jobscan’s 98% F500 ATS claim)
- **Top third** of the page is the money zone
- Skills as easy bullets; experience bullets start with an action verb and a number
- Steal JD keywords into résumé **and** cover letter
- If a posting wants Splunk (etc.), go learn it — don’t fake it (agrees Jachja)
- Break past jobs into **basic skill terms**; show you’ll keep learning

**Module 2** (help desk / IT support):

- Two readers: ATS (titles, tools, certs) then a human skim
- Human checks: do they understand the role, is it readable, is non-IT work **translated**, can they talk to users
- Strong = scannable, **targeted**, plain language (they treat clear writing as a job skill), tech **and** transferable, **progress** (training / labs / certs in flight)
- Don’t exaggerate. Translate: escalations, documenting issues, process/inventory, cross-team fixes
- Flashy design is unnecessary

**7 tips (on-page related):** map customer service → help desk, communication → users/teams, Office → IT productivity; put that on the résumé + LinkedIn; cover letter explains the pivot; CompTIA A+/Net+/Sec+; home labs (Linux, etc.); industry-crossover jobs. **5 IT jobs:** certs + career services mention only.

## 13.4 Tensions / experienced candidate
