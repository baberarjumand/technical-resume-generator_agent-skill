### 2.1 Criteria for success (verbatim principles)

- It takes **less than 60 seconds** for the resume to convince an employer you are qualified for the **target job**.
- Show a **select** group of skills and experiences that match those required.
- Organization and formatting help the reader **find** the qualifying information.
- Experiences are **concrete and quantified**.
- **Absolutely no typos or errors.**

### 2.2 Resume vs CV (as contrasted by MIT)

| | Resume | CV |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Goal | Shows how experiences and skills qualify you for the **target job** | Shows academic achievements and **research qualifications** |
| Length | **Strict 2-page (1 sheet of paper) limit** | 2–3 pages for a graduate student; grows over a career |
| Typical sections | Name & contact; Education; Work experience; Skills | Name & contact; Education; Research experience; Career objectives/research interests; Publications & presentations; Awards & grants; Professional societies; Teaching; References |

MIT treats a CV as “a special kind of resume” for academic/research roles. In the article, “resume” means either unless they contrast the two.

**Implication for this project:** Industry SWE/frontend roles → resume (UNC one-pager is even stricter than MIT’s 2-page max). Academic/research applications → CV with publications/teaching.

### 2.3 Audience and tailoring

- Resume + cover letter are the **first** materials read; their job is to move you to an interview, not tell your whole life story.
- Tailor to the job and, if possible, to the **specific readers**.
- If requirements are explicit, make it **obvious** you meet them.
- Custom resume **for every application**:
  1. Read the posting; list required qualifications.
  2. Highlight matching skills/accomplishments by: including different experiences, putting **more bullets** under the most relevant roles, and **moving important experiences earlier**.
- Space is limited; be critical about what to leave out.
- Example: “drive independent research” vs “work closely with an interdisciplinary team” demand different evidence.

### 2.4 Concretize and quantify (worked rewrites)

MIT’s before → after pairs:

| Weak | Strong |
| --- | --- |
| Researched sensors and instrumentation in the Canty lab | Developed method for tracking air conditioner efficiency from harmonic content in demanded electricity; co-advised 8 graduate students; contributing author on 5 papers |
| Teaching Assistant for several classes | Organized bi-weekly laboratory assignments; taught technical workshops for 3 lecture and lab-based courses |
| Treasurer for Tau Beta Pi | Managed $6K budget and raised $500 |
| Volunteer with regional young leadership board | Chair of nominating committee; recruited 60+ young leaders in 3 years; wrote first committee charter / bylaws |
| Developed relationships with new sponsors | Recruited $10k in new sponsorship |
| Served as a summer research advisor for RSI | Mentored a high school RSI student; designed a parallel union-find project; work selected as a top-10 finalist of 80 |

**Verb quality:** Prefer impact verbs (Designed, Managed, Led, Developed) over passive ones (Participated, Researched).

**Research-specific:**
- Brief lab overview is OK; **individual contribution is required**.
- For research positions, **include the PI’s name** (academic pedigree).

### 2.5 Skimmability

- Recruiters skim in **30–60 seconds**.
- **Headers should package experience for the job** (add Teaching or Entrepreneurship headers when relevant).
- **Order headers** so the most relevant section is first.
- White space, indentation, bullets. Dense ≠ more convincing; a spacious resume may communicate more.
- **Bold important words** so they can be found quickly.

### 2.6 Proofread

A single mistake can dump you in the “no” pile. Use a detail-oriented second reader.

### 2.7 What the two annotated MIT samples teach

**Annotated CV page (academic appointments first)**  
Callouts printed on the sample:

- Clearly defined headers and sections
- Academic job search → put **academic appointments early** and expand detail
- Use **action verbs** to convey action and impact (highlighted in yellow on verbs: Manage, Lead, Develop, Design, Advise, Organize, teach, Mentor, develop, Generated, led)
- **Concretize** accomplishments (red highlights on distinctive technical/context phrases: flagship MIT & Masdar collaboration, counteract heat islanding, 400 MW, root-cause fault detection, U.S. Navy ships, etc.)
- **Quantify** transferable impact (blue: bi-weekly, three courses, 2 Ph.D. + 6 M.S. + 1 undergrad, ~20 coaching sessions, 20 students)

Layout notes: serif name; two contact clusters (mailing address vs website/email/phone); education with dissertation/thesis/advisor; academic appointments with mentor name; communication-lab advising as a second role.

**Annotated 2-column resume (industry / technical consulting)**  
Banner on the sample: “Resume for technical consulting job application — Concise and highlighting technical, management, people, and communication skills and experiences.”

- Left column: Academic & Research Experience, then Industry Experience (Ambarcbi / construction estimating — shows non-lab work can stay if it shows scoped delivery)
- Right column: compact contact with icons, Education (degree + school + years only), Awards, Engineering & Research Interests (keyword list), Technical Skills **banded by level** (Advanced / Intermediate / Basic), Select Publications
- Same color language: yellow verbs, blue numbers, red concrete details
- 2-column is called out in the article as **unusual** but useful for more sections + white space
- Skills are proficiency-tiered, not a flat alphabetized dump
