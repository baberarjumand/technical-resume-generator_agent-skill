**LinkedIn AI summary (page header)**

Knowing what to highlight means showcasing technical skills, measurable achievements, and relevant experience so recruiters see impact fast.

- Show measurable impact (process, cost, engagement).
- Detail project involvement: what you built, tools, outcomes.
- Tailor to the JD: most relevant technologies and achievements.

## 9.1 Posts on this page (primary)

### Shubham Saboo — AI/ML resumes (Senior AI PM @ Google; “reviewed 2000+ resumes”)

Seven tips aimed at **AI/ML** roles:

1. **End-to-end project work:** concept → data → model → validation → **deployment**. Full lifecycle, not a notebook.
2. **Quantify real-world impact**, not theory. Examples: “Reduced customer churn by 20% through predictive modeling”; “Increased sales by 15% with a recommendation system.”
3. **Cross-functional collaboration** (data engineers, PMs, stakeholders) with specific instances.
4. **Deployment:** Docker, Kubernetes, AWS/GCP/Azure — with examples and impact.
5. **Open source:** PRs, issues resolved, role on known projects.
6. **Recent tech:** LLMs, RL, generative AI — only if you actually used them.
7. **Keep current:** courses/tech you learned; keep that section updated.

### Prateek Gogia — Amazon/Apple screening patterns (SEM @ Apple; 200+ apps)

Aimed at **fresh graduates**, but the honesty rule applies to everyone:

- Impact **with duration**: “Led 6-month capstone… +35% engagement” beats “Worked on a project.”
- **2–4 languages/tools** you can discuss in an interview, not 15 frameworks.
- Depth over breadth (React, Docker, Spark mastered > laundry list).
- Interviewers **will dig into every listed skill**. Tutorial-only Kubernetes/Docker → **leave it off**. Overselling hurts more than a shorter list.
- What matters: progression, honest self-assessment, finished projects, collaboration/communication. Curiosity > knowing every technology.

### Macy So — PM/program-manager skim (ex-Microsoft)

Recruiters often stop after **~6 seconds**:

1. Instead of a prose summary, **3–4 “Key Impact” bullets** of biggest measurable wins (e.g. +30% efficiency / 5 global teams; $2M cost savings).
2. Metrics for everything (adoption, timelines, efficiency, retention — not only revenue).
3. Mini **portfolio link** (Notion, GitHub, public dashboard).
4. Show **influence**, not just headcount (aligned eng/marketing/ops across 3 continents…).
5. Optional **failure → learning** mini-bullet with a number (40% → 95% adoption in 2 months).
6. Stronger verbs: orchestrated, accelerated, scaled, influenced, optimized, launched.
7. JD keywords for ATS **and** humans (Agile, OKRs, Jira, cloud).
8. Subtle visual hierarchy: bold headings; she also suggests **small icons / shading** — **conflicts with TIH/Yale ATS (no graphics/icons)**. Prefer bold + whitespace, skip icons.

### Brian Woolfrey — Amazon senior / STAR + numbers (technical recruiter)

Vague “Led development of payment processing system” fails. Want:

- Architecture + **fault-tolerant** system that: −47% transaction failures; 1M+ daily txns; −$2.3M processing cost; CSAT 3.2 → 4.7.
- **STAR + numbers:** Situation, Task, Action (what **you** did), Result (measurable). Checkout example: bottleneck (S) / 40% load-time cut needed (T) / Redis + query work (A) / −63% load time + $4.2M Q3 revenue (R).
- Quantify latency, scale (team, budget, users).
- For Amazon: map to Leadership Principles (Customer Obsession, Ownership, Deliver Results).
- Avoid generic responsibilities and missing metrics.

### Eyvanna Connole — technical recruiter (HIRECLOUT; juniors → principals)

Resume is first impression **and** first filter.

- Lead with impact: “Optimized data pipeline using Spark, reducing processing time by 40%.”
- Don’t stop at “Python, AWS, Docker.” Say **what you built with Python**, **which AWS services** (EC2/Lambda/S3), product vs internal tool vs automation.
- Tailor language to the JD.
- Clean headings + bullets; skim-friendly.
- Story: problems solved, tools used, value delivered.

### Eli Gündüz — AU/NZ tech CVs (recruiter-side; Careersy)

Habits that “age” a candidate:

| Cut | Replace with |
| --- | --- |
| “References available on request” | One-line achievement (“Scaled fintech app 2k → 50k users in 12 months”) |
| Career objective (“seeking a challenging role”) | Sharp summary: industry + stack + value (“5+ years cloud engineering, specialising in AWS cost optimisation”) |
| Duties copied from a JD | Measurable outcomes / consequences + results |

Coaching rewrite example:

- Before: “Set strategy and led execution for stateless hosting golden paths via the IDP.”
- After: “Delivered a 3-year stateless hosting strategy via the IDP, adopted by **14 engineering squads** across APAC. Reduced deployment time by **60%** and unlocked **$2.5M** annual infra savings.”

Other AU/NZ notes (not US-default):

- He says **single-column, 4 pages max** — **conflicts with US 1-page norm** (TIH, Yale undergrad, “Why US clients prefer short”). For candidates targeting US/remote FAANG-style: **one page**.
- Last **5 years** of relevant roles; older work → “extended career history.”
- Two things beat design: facts in plain English in **10 seconds**; apply where those facts matter.

Site/testimonials (commercial): `https://lnkd.in/gW7Equtj`.

### Kevin J. Dabulis — 20+ years IT (30 years recruiting)

Résumé is a **highlight reel**, not a history book.

- Lead with **who you are now**, not 2005 chronology. Example: “IT leader specializing in digital transformation, cloud architecture, and infrastructure modernization.”
- Results not tasks: “Migrated 100+ applications to Azure, cutting deployment time by 40%.”
- **Condense the first decade** under “Prior Experience.”
- Use **current language** even if you manage people who do the work (Kubernetes, Terraform, CI/CD, AI Ops) — ATS is real.
- Drop “references upon request” and ancient certs.

### Jen Beatty — iOS before/after (landed interview at a major iOS shop)

| Before (task + stack) | After (ownership + why + impact) |
| --- | --- |
| Create robust system of modules with Swift Package Manager | **Architected** modular components with SPM, enabling the team to **scale development** and ship tools/features in isolation |
| Integrate C++ libraries for Bluetooth products | Integrated C++ for 3rd-party BLE **and developed custom BLE libraries**, letting users create/use **custom profiles** |
| Build reactive caching layer with CoreData, Combine, Swift Concurrency | Built reactive caching framework… **improving API usability** and **enabling offline** |

Change: action-oriented ownership, **why** the tech was used, user/team impact. Not a stack dump.

### Joshua Talreja — Staff+ / hiring-manager 6-second scan (Airbnb India eng hiring; Google/Microsoft TA)

Hiring managers look for: **operate at scale**, **multiply impact**, **make me look good**. Claims 90% of 500+ resumes fail all three.

1. Responsibility dump → “Led **12 engineers** to reduce deployment time by **73%**, enabling **$2M** in faster feature releases.”
2. Vague “improved performance” → “Redesigned caching **400ms → 40ms** → **+25%** engagement → **$3M ARR**.”
3. Always include **why it mattered** (problem → solution → business change).
4. Tech laundry list is skimmed; **weave stack into stories**: “Built React + GraphQL platform serving 10M users…”
5. Staff+ is multiplying others, architecture, technical strategy — resume must prove altitude.

**Template he publishes:** `[Action Verb] + [What] + [How] + [Quantified Impact] + [Business Outcome]`  
Example: “Architected microservices migration strategy across 15 teams using DDD, reducing deployment dependencies by 80% and enabling teams to ship 3x faster.”

### Essential Elements of a Tech Job Resume

[https://www.linkedin.com/top-content/career/resume-formats-for-tech-jobs/essential-elements-of-a-tech-job-resume/](https://www.linkedin.com/top-content/career/resume-formats-for-tech-jobs/essential-elements-of-a-tech-job-resume/)

AI summary: measurable impact; only tools you can discuss; simple formatting; no buzzword clutter.

**Shreya Mehta (ex-Amazon/Microsoft recruiter; 1,000+ resumes) — 5 sections for 6-figure interviews**

1. **Positioning statement (3 lines):** who you are / problems you solve / where. Example: “Backend engineer with 4 years… scaling infra at early-stage startups. Shipped distributed systems handling 50M+ requests/day. Currently focused on latency, observability, and developer experience.” Drop “motivated team player.”
2. **Experience as case study:** hired to do / actually built / what changed. “Redesigned caching → −47% API latency across 3 services.”
3. **Company/team context** (esp. big-co): “Ads ML Infrastructure team at Meta, supporting $XXB annual revenue.”
4. **Projects optional** except new/career-switch: 1–2 shipped projects with users/error rates, not “Built a web app using React and Node.”
5. **Skills that support the story** — reinforce in bullets.

**Jonathan Corrales — 15-second hiring-manager checklist (15 years reviewing tech resumes)**

Contact: email, full name, phone; optional portfolio/LinkedIn; timezone if remote; city/state if onsite/hybrid.  
Skills: exact tech needed or similar; **one skills section**; anything they’re considering trying.  
Experienced: relevant titles, business impact, dates as **month + year**, tech from skills section, company + location, years.  
Entry: internships, OSS, personal projects in relevant tech, transferable work.  
Education/certs only if needed. Work auth / clearance when relevant.  
Final: sense they can do the work; **&lt;3 grammar issues; &lt;3 typos**; well formatted; **under 3 pages (preferred 1–2)**; don’t overuse underline/bold/italic.

**Atharva Joshi (AWS ML kernel) — early-career**

Simple format: avoid heavy horizontal rules, text walls, excessive bold; **one page**. Don’t only list tools or the business problem — say **what the service did and which decisions you made**. Don’t invent 12%→95% accuracy. Clarity &gt; CUDA buzzwords. First-round readers often aren’t ML specialists.

**Crystal Rehling — recruiter basics**

Full first+last name; **city, state, zip** (searchable); timezone even for remote; email/phone/LinkedIn URL; **no photo** on resume (photo on LinkedIn). Filename = `Firstname Lastname`. Summary not objective; hard data not soft skills. IT: **Core Technical Skills immediately after summary**. Recent roles get the most bullets; 10+ years old → highlights or title/dates only. **Education last** unless new grad. Keep LinkedIn as complete as the resume (disagrees with “keep LinkedIn brief”).

**Ashley Perriman (Quora / ex-Google/Meta recruiter) — 6-second reject**

#1 mistake: **pasting the JD as “accomplishments.”** Framework: **Action + Outcome + Proof**. Before/after: “Worked on API performance” → “Reduced API p95 420ms → 180ms by optimizing queries + caching (−57%).”

## 9.3 Conflicts vs earlier sources

- **Length:** US/IT posts and TIH → **1 page**. Jonathan Corrales allows **1–2, max 3**. Eli Gündüz (AU/NZ) → **4 pages max**. Default for this repo remains **one page** for industry SWE.
- **Years of history:** Eli last **5 years**; Kevin/Gulrukh last **10–15**; Crystal: 10+ year-old jobs as title/dates only. For a mid-level SWE (~6–8 years SWE): show the full SWE arc; drop ancient non-tech unless it has a metric.
- **Skills list:** TIH/Jachja/Yale want a scannable skills block; Joshua/Khushbu say skills are skimmed and proof in bullets matters more. Keep **2–3 short categories** *and* prove them in bullets. Prateek: only skills you can defend live.
- **Graphics:** Macy’s icons/shading vs TIH/Yale/Christine Stepp **no icons/tables**. No icons.
- **Zip code:** Crystal wants city/state/**zip**; FTL/Yale often omit zip. City + country/timezone is enough for most remote candidates.
- **Education position:** Crystal/TIH experienced → education last. UNC/Yale student templates → education first.
- **STAR (Brian)** vs WHO/X-Y-Z/APR: compatible. Prefer **Action + Outcome + Proof** (Ashley) plus **business outcome** (Joshua) plus **tools** (Jachja/Eyvanna).
- **Company context line** (Shreya) is extra vs UNC templates — useful when the employer isn’t famous; optional for well-known logos.
- **Failure→learning bullet** (Macy): optional; don’t spend scarce one-page space unless the turnaround is impressive.
- **Amazon LPs** (Brian): only when applying to Amazon.

## 9.4 What to take for an experienced candidate

Headline/positioning (Shreya/TIH) + skills after it (Crystal/Jachja) + experience as case studies with **scope, baseline→result, tools, and business why**. One page. No skill you can’t defend. Weave React/TypeScript/etc. into bullets, not only the skills line. Optional GitHub/portfolio proof link. Education at the bottom.
