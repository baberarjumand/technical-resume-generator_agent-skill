Teal uses “CV” in the US-industry sense (close to “resume”). Treat length advice as 1–2 pages, which aligns with UNC’s one-pager for students and MIT’s resume (not academic CV).

### 3.1 Framing

A CS CV should work like a tuned algorithm: skills, experience, and achievements arranged to solve the employer’s problem (hiring the right person). Balance **technical ability** with **business/strategic impact**. Emphasize analytical expertise for software development, data analysis, or IT management.

### 3.2 Writing rules Teal lists up front

- **Highlight certifications and specializations early** (examples they name: CompTIA, CCNA, CISSP; cybersecurity, data science, software development).
- **Quantify impact** (their stock examples: 30% efficiency from a software optimization; 50% less downtime from IT management).
- **Tailor to the job description**; surface cloud/ML/etc. when the posting does.
- **Detail tech proficiency:** languages (Python, Java, C++), data tools, cloud platforms.
- **Show soft skills and leadership briefly:** teamwork, explaining technical ideas simply.

### 3.3 Essential vs optional sections

**Essential**
1. Personal statement (short summary of qualifications, technical expertise, career goals)
2. Career experience (responsibilities + achievements per role)
3. Education (CS-related degrees)
4. Certifications (CISSP, CEH, Oracle, etc.)
5. Skills (Python, Java, and other concrete tools)

**Optional**
1. Professional affiliations (ACM, IEEE Computer Society)
2. Projects (software you built or contributed to)
3. Awards and honors
4. Publications
5. Continuing education (courses/seminars)

**Structure tips**
- Logical flow: statement → experience → rest
- Highlight key achievements **early inside each section**
- Reverse chronological roles
- Professional, precise language
- Combination format: skills + reverse-chronological history
- 1–2 pages; quality over quantity

**Conflict with UNC samples:** UNC student templates **omit a personal statement** and often put **education first**. Teal’s examples are written for mid-career personas and open with a summary. For an experienced hire, Teal’s summary-first model is closer; for intern/new-grad, UNC’s education-first model is the one UNC actually shows.

### 3.4 Formatting

- Clean, uncluttered, consistent margins and fonts (“like clean code”).
- Bullets under each role that name **specific languages and systems**.
- Project-based achievements (“led a mobile app that gained 10,000 users”; “ML improved processing 40%”).
- Technical skills should be easy to scan; interpersonal skills get their own grouping if included.

### 3.5 Personal statements (good vs bad)

**Strong pattern:** years of experience + 2–3 specialty domains + one proof of impact + what you want to do next. Example shape: “Analytical … 5+ years in software development, data analysis, and ML. Track record designing software that improved functionality and UX. Seeking to bring … to a dynamic team.”

**Weak pattern:** “I am a Computer Scientist with experience in coding… I enjoy problem-solving and am looking for a new place to apply my skills.”

**How to stand out:** concise, quantified, tailored to the job’s industry problem.

### 3.6 Experience bullets (good vs bad)

**Strong:** Developed and implemented a data management system that increased retrieval speed **35%**; led 5 developers on an app that increased engagement **50%** in six months; introduced ML that improved predictive accuracy **25%**.

**Weak:** “Worked on data management and software development projects.” / “Participated in the creation of an application and contributed to team meetings.”

**Another strong cluster:** Orchestrated cloud migration (−30% cost, +20% uptime); cybersecurity −40% vulnerabilities; AI chatbot −60% response time, +15% CSAT.

**How to stand out:** quantify; name projects; tailor to DS, SWE, cloud, AI; name technologies and outcomes.

### 3.7 Skills section design

Split **technical expertise** from **interpersonal/collaboration**:

Technical: programming & coding; data structures & algorithms; software development & testing; database management (SQL, MongoDB).

Interpersonal: team collaboration; problem-solving; communication to non-technical stakeholders; adaptability to new tech.

Align both to the posting; quantify where possible; illustrate with real examples.

### 3.8 Tailoring checklist

- Emphasize projects/experiences that match the posting (SWE vs other).
- Mirror job-posting language for ATS (e.g. “machine learning”, “data structures”).
- Put the most relevant skills and certifications first.
- Align the professional summary to the role.
- Do not skip soft skills/teamwork in tech.

### 3.9 Example personas on the page (structure only)

Nine labeled examples (Computer Science, Fresher, Entry Level, Computer Scientist, Student, Graduate, plus overlapping variants). Recurring shape:

- Header: name, phone, email, LinkedIn
- 3–5 line summary
- Three jobs, newest first, 3 bullets each, every bullet has a %
- Skills list that restates the bullets
- B.S. Computer Science
- 2–3 certifications (CISSP, Azure, Oracle/AWS)

**Use with caution:** the examples are heavily templatized (repeated 30% / 20% / 40% metrics, similar company-name fiction). Steal the **bullet grammar**, not the fake numbers or the implication that every junior needs CISSP.
